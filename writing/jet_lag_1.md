# A Ramble on House-Ruling Jet Lag Hide & Seek

So a bunch of my friends in I took the chance to play Jet Lag Hide & Seek in Chicago over the Juneteenth holiday, and while it's still fresh in my minds, I want to discuss our general approach to trying to have fun with it, and how we maybe messed up.

## The Setup

We had originally played a smaller game starting with the stock cardset the year prior, still in Chicago, small board, and cut it off at about half the city's volume. We exempted the Closest Airport because a north/south divide was good from a gameplay perspective, and because, frankly, it was fun.

We did ban METRA for the sake of sanity, and due to service times.  We wanted to keep it simple.

This time however, we included the expansion card set.  This was not inherently the problem; I suspect our *methodology* was. When we had initially played, we removed fairly few curses, versus with the expansion card set, we removed quite a bit.

We recognized a lot of the expansion cards are good for *watching*, but frankly not fun to *play* for a wide gamut of our players.  So we removed some.

## Mistake #1 - Deck Alterations

We didn't pay attention to the *ratio* of curses to time bonuses though.

This meant that compared to our first game the year prior, each team effectively only used one curse in each round. This is, to say the least, kinda bunk!  We want the curses, and we went through the curses to make sure we all were on board with the ones we got (or at least a majority of players on both teams wanted each individual one.)

The expansion set, or perhaps the default set with medium games, includes 36 minute bonuses. The other team got *two* of these*.  While these may not be strictly related, they're both the problem of deck balance.

IF YOU ARE ALTERING THE DECK, BE SURE TO PRESERVE THE RATIO

## Mistake #2 - Team Balancing Is Hard

Our first game, we had a local transit fiend, an out-of-town transit fiend, and myself (transit oriented, but not an expert on the CTA.)  

This time, it was three locals versus two somewhat recently moved folks and one out-of-towner, with ~5y of "Living in the region" experience and 20y on the other team.

This is 100% on our part, and we should have realized this going on.  

When Sam, Adam, and Ben play Hide & Seek, it's on an area they're largely unfamiliar with, or across such an enormous area that any regional local knowledge is small in the grand scheme of the game.

We... did not do this.

Do not be like us.

## Mistake 3 - Assymetric Information War

This is really part of #2, and was specific to our game, but it's such a frustrating experience.

In the second round, the more experienced team asked for a station photo, and while we did have a rules adjudicator on standby, we thought our photo was good.  It was *not*.  It showed enough details to SEVERELY narrow down the playing field.

This, in isolation, isn't a big deal.  Blunders like that are part of the game.  However, our team had *no idea* they knew this, and watched them getting closer and closer for the better part of 45m. The game was *so much shorter* it's not even funny.

To see your opponents approaching and not understand *how* they narrowed it down is awful. We thought nearly two thirds of the city was still in play, but that one photo had narrowed it down so dramatically that we had doomed ourselves.

## Will This Be Fun?

If you are playing Hide & Seek with these rules, I urge you to *explicitly* ask, "is this fun?"

Super lopsided teams with no chance to rebalance is *not super fun!*  Greater than stock RNG over such a small round size is *not fun!*

We want the game to be interesting, to be exciting, and frankly to be just the right amount of challenging.


Anyways, thanks for reading.  I hope this serves you well should you decide to GO GO GO.
