Sakura Kyōko was not easily roused. Eh, in less polite words? Fine; she slept like the dead.

The dead dead, not the fake dead like she was; not bad for a girl who cheated death on a daily basis, eh?

She stared down at her hands, still a little bruised from her scuffle the night before, a stupid grin on her face, as if to say you should’a seen the other guy.

Speaking absolutely, the wraiths never stood a chance. Sakura Kyōko was a magical girl, but she had more experience in her field than most tenured academics. Sure, Tomoe Mami had bigger guns, and Akemi Homura was prettier, but she was tough and nobody could take that from her. She had stumbled into bed during the witching hour, that was all.

She glanced over and saw her baby sister still sleeping soundly, very aware that if she groaned too loud she’d wake up Momo.

Can’t wake ’er, gotta be cool.

She slid on socks, a few holes in them still, but still much warmer than they used to be. Th’ hell am I doing I can just– she began, about to transform into her work uniform, pausing only because she thought better of it.

The light show might wake ’er.

She grabbed a few cubes from her countertop, serving as paperweight for the warning underneath. On a ripped-out sheet of notebook paper lay a warning to supplement her tirade, in chicken-scratch handwriting.

“DO NOT TOUCH – KYOUKO”

Part of her name was crossed through; what type of girl forgot how to spell her own name?

She held the cubes in her left hand as the despair was siphoned away, shoving closed fist, cubes, soul, trimmed-too-short fingernails, through the armhole of a bra. Then, the same feat through the sleeves of a worn green hoodie. Yes, that hoodie, why’d’a care what a girl wears?

It was always comfortable and it smelled like her; why bother with anything different?

She gingerly opened the door, grateful the hinges hadn’t been enthusiastic in singing her a dissonant choir of wails. Stupid door always woke Momo up.

She slowly but surely hit the bathroom, and wandered into the kitchenette attached to the church. It was the quietest it’d be all day, still able to hear the birds outside in the cemetery; there was service today, after all.

Her father wandered in, his beard unkempt as her mother followed in row, swatting at him playfully.

“Joji, I told you to comb your beard before service, you’re going to be confused for a beggar!”

“Is that such a problem dear? We all serve the same mission after all.” he mused, Michiru rolling her eyes as she set up camp in front of the stove.

She only made use of a measly two burners for breakfast, embarrassingly restrained when compared to the full expanse of the range, but then again, she was cooking for her congregation, not her husband’s today.

The elder Sakura sat down at the head of the table, Joji quietly thumbing through papers and a worn pile of books for a few minutes. He never did quite manage to finish his sermons on time, after all. All the while, Michiru attentively manned the stove, and after a few minutes, slid a plate of something steaming and delicious at him.

He received the meal with a warmth that, as corny as it was, made Kyōko feel safe. She had seen that smile, that lingering tenderness a thousand times before; that same quiet love was around her whole childhood, it mattered. Even when times got tough, he still had his smile.

Kyōko realized she’d been watching all the while, looming.

Damn, gotta be less creepy. Huntin’ wraiths all the time has me loomin’ ominously. Aura farming or somethin’. Wouldn’t need to hunt so much if— Nah. Not the time.

She couldn’t help but to give them both a look as she sat down at the table, still careful to not make too much noise.

Kyōko’s plate had already been set, and there was bread on the table, snagging a piece with the fervor of a street vagrant. Her father returned her look with yet another, a knowing glance serving as riposte as she began to devour breakfast.

Aw shit.

Kyōko awkwardly stopped chewing, food still lodged partially in her esophagus. Father Sakura hesitated in his response, trying to divine whether the redness blooming on her face was embarrassment or injury.

No, no, that isn’t right, he thought to himself. It wasn’t up to him to judge his daughter, She would do that in due time. Even then, he wasn’t especially worried for his beloved daughter. Every story, myth, testimonial of the girls who interacted with Her described her as exceptionally kind and forgiving; She was a goddess after all.

“Kyōko….” he said quietly, his voice absent the tone that carried to the last row of the pews with clarity and–

You’re not here for the religious testimonials? But I thought you wanted a story about Madoka? Is this story too different? Fine.

Kyōko gulped, the look on her face decidedly more guilty than she would have been had she been choking. Food was a point of contention for the family, or at least, it used to be. It had been less serious as of late, but old habits die hard.

“Sorry Pa, I was hungry hungry. You know how patrols get me starving.”

He smiled through a quiet sigh, the lingering pride of his daughter’s duties more than enough to offset the weight of her faux-paus; it wasn’t like the goddess would show up to dinner.

“Well if you still have duty on the mind, would you say the blessing then?” he offered to his daughter; Kyōko cracked her knuckles and reached out to hold hands.

Easy.

“Blessed be the goddess, who protects our home and hearth.”

May she keep my family safe, and Momo happy. Please.

“Blessed be the goddess, keeper of our obligation and hope, vanquisher of despair by Her own hands and those that work in her stead.”

May wraiths show up so I’m not chasing ghosts with nothing to show for it.

“Blessed be the goddess, may Her radiance be eternal and Her wisdom be observed, in pursuit of a more luminous future.”

May she show me how to fix things. How to make amends for the pain I’ve caused.

“Amen.”

“Amen.”

Kyōko’s hands dropped, finishing her own prayer. Shortly after, her eyes locked on the cornbread at the center of the table as though it were wraiths. She had a feast to enjoy.

It didn’t get much better than this, she thought to herself, her smile toothy as crumbs fell from her face.

Unexpectedly, end of sentence.

Well, girls didn’t wake up anywhere but the Law after that.

Right. Back to work.

Girlfriend.

She could be more precise, she should, but no language had an elegant, concise way to express the complexities of their relationship.

Actually, in hindsight, girlfriend probably was the closest term. Their souls had mingled on the cosmic plane, for Goddess’ sake!

Did she actually just? I miss when I was with Mami-san… She wouldn’t do that to me.

Gertrud had said it with a wink, that rascal!

Charlotte ran her finger across the stone inset and shivered; pins and needles, had she always been this sensitive?

“But… how?”

Gertrud turned to look at Nagisa and plucked a single flower from the bramble growing around her arms.

“Surely you know about girls with thorns. Do we know of any sharp, pointed girls who might be able to produce illusions?”

Illusions? No, I heard it break, I felt it break. I was… gone. I remember nothing, a great heaping void of nothing.

No Law, no space, no time, no n-th color windings of fate. Just… nothing.

Gertrud bent over to put the flower in Charlotte’s hair, the bramble forming scrunchies to retain her ponytails.

“There’s uh… a few girls in the Law that spring to mind—”

“How about who called Kazamino home? Ringing any bells?”

Kazamino had only ever had one girl with the power to produce illusions, and she was very much not in the Law.

What do you mean Kyoko-san was the one who helped me get shattered… and Mami-san… Both of them? Do they hate me now? Is this some sick joke?

“Wait, you mean—”

“Yes, though she doesn’t know it at the moment.”

Charlotte’s jaw hung agape; how strange to see the miscreant not filling it with cheese for once.

“How could she not know it‽”

The pair wandered through the garden birdcage as they pruned and weeded, plucked and trimmed. They had much to discuss.

Hitomi rapped her fist on the door of a classmate for the second time in just as many days. What she didn’t expect was to find that her final pattern popped the door open.

She bit her tongue, held her breath, and found… no one standing behind it.

She stared at the open door incredulously, glancing left then right, only to realize the street was deserted.

Her eyes drifted back to the door, split on the threshold. She knew better, she was raised better than this, better than breaking-and-entering (what an Americanism!)

Oh, I really shouldn’t, but… if something is wrong I need to know sooner rather than later.

She creaked the door open just a bit more, closing it with a soft thud. She slipped her shoes off, borrowing the powder-blue slippers that under more mundane circumstances would be used by her classmate. Figures, Shizuki-san trying to be respectful even in disrespectfully breaking-and-entering.

Where has she gotten off to? I’m starting to get seriously worried! First someone body-snatches Homura-chan… now?

Curious though. Do both of her parents work?

If the apples are this bad…

It slammed shut.

The final missing subject was none other than Kaname Madoka.

What about the photos in the living room? What ab–o-oh.

It was the one used at Sayaka’s funeral. God, why did she have to keep this?

What was Shizuki Hitomi meddling in? And why didn’t she seem to want to contract yet?

Homura didn’t want to walk. She had wings for Goddess’ sake, and yet, here she was, leading Wagamama, Ibari, and Ganko across town. The timestop had forced her hand, and so she held theirs, like she was their guardian escorting gradeschoolers across Mitakihara.

Though she’d be reticent to admit as much if pressed, she’d have said she felt bad leaving them alone.

They were part of her, could she really discard parts of herself like that so easily? Doubly so, it seemed especially risky when she wasn’t entirely sure of the source of the timestop.

Was it me? Or was my magic stolen by a wraith again, mutant and altered?

No, surely it would have made itself known if it took enough of myself to do so. That means the guilty party must be someone else.

But who?

She paused at the crossing, despite no cars being present. She could have sworn this intersection was busier, and yet…

Her party passed through the familiar shopping district, Homura’s footfall halting immediately as Wagamama, Ibari, and Ganko bumped into her, and then one another, in that order.

Hm.

The Class Representative Witch, the Birdcage Witch, The Sweets Witch, The Rose Garden Witch, countless names, countless fights; functional, consistent, and utterly boring.

This wasn’t her costume.

White. Like a wedding dress.

She had no time for vanity.

This is much easier when I don’t have to make my own pipebombs… Though, really? Skypiercers?
