### Of Pitch and Pralines

Add sugar, spice, everything nice to taste. 

After a few hours in the oven, are those magical girls still running?

---

“–we’re screwed.” Sayaka huffed, the wind knocked out of her lungs by the fall. *Damn.*

Homura was slower to rise, her knees and elbows scuffed from the entrance. It was one thing to be sucked into a fantastical world of magical girls and strange enemies (again, apparently), it was quite another to be *literally* sucked into a pocket universe that smelled like a candy store.

When was the last time she’d had sweets, anyway?

Sayaka offered a hand, somewhat shocked that Homura was injured, even a little. She’s a magical girl, she’s supposed to be built of tougher stuff! Unless. Unless, unless, unless…. Does she *not know what happens to magical girls? Does she still think she’s a person?*

“How screwed are we?” Homura murmured, the word a little alien on her tongue. *Screwed*. Damned, cursed, doomed, ruined, a thousand other equally terminal outcomes, all collapsed into one.

“Historically, Mami-san was the one to defeat this witch. Mami and…”

She paused. Telling Homura about killing witches might make her upset again. 

"Well… somehow I doubt she’s gonna help us out this time.”

“She tried to kill you.”

“Yep. Though to be fair, we did get along, once upon a time.” She opted not to mention that their last “project” together had been a failed attempt to stop Homura from ripping Madoka from the Law of Cycles. “But a witch?" she continued. "There shouldn’t be *any*, much less *this* one!”

“Witch? Historically?” Homura murmured, wringing her hands nervously. Yes, she was a magical girl (apparently), but the technicolor wasteland that laid before her was still unlike anything she had ever seen. Costumes and healing were one thing, *this* was another entirely.

“Okay so, the bad part. When a magical girl dies, a witch is born.”

“A witch.” The words tumbled out of her mouth, more familiar the more she reflected on the term.  It had a weight to her, a gravity, one that seemed to drag her down with it as the thought descended further and further into her psyche. 

“All the parts of her life, the things that hurt, all the sadness, anger, despair… it catches up to her.  And fast.”

“I thought the enemies of magical girls were those strange monks, not beings who used to be magical girls!”

“I… thought you didn’t know what a wraith was?”

Homura’s face twisted, surprising even herself in the moment. How *did* she know what a wraith was? *Had* she seen one?

Her vision flickered, the daedal labyrinth replaced with a detached viewport, a screen by any other name, filled with the image of a towering white robe. She felt the distortion of the wraith’s face, visual noise to accompany the static in her head, backed by ominous droning like a bassline. 

She felt something on her shoulder, pawing, notched the string of a bow and— 

“I remember fighting them.”

Sayaka relaxed for a moment, unable to stop herself. *We’re getting closer.*

“We’re not fighting wraiths today, we’re trying to end a witch.”

“But she… used to be a magical girl…?”

“To be honest, it’s a mercy killing more than anything. Even if they didn’t drop grief seeds, it’s the right thing to do…”

Homura stared at her, as though her head had burst, as though she’d grown a mermaid’s tail. Sayaka seemed to have resigned herself to the situation, the alleged inevitability of magical girls becoming witches. Homura was appalled; how could she be okay with that outcome? What happened to *justice!?*

Homura hadn’t even *seen* a witch yet, but she had read enough stories to know that witches aren’t described keenly. They’re burned, drowned, hanged. Even when they’re innocent, *especially* when they’re innocent. 

She felt her heart pounding in her chest, the mostly-dissolved sutures beginning to ache. The idea that witches were once magical girls was nauseating, and it was, more than anything… the truth. She knew it to be absolutely true on a level she couldn’t explain, the shape of the tragedy fitting perfectly into a hole in her memories. Unfortunately, that very recollection had illuminated countless other gaps, their contents still shrouded in mystery.

“Is that our fate too? Is that *my* fate?”

It felt so wrong to look at her and see such a frail girl, so unsure of herself. Akemi Homura had suffered at the hands of fate, deplorable as it was, but she had never been unsure of who she was. She had a singular mission, a singular objective, to save Kaname Madoka, as misguided as her methods may be. And then Madoka had stolen even that from her.

Sayaka couldn’t even tell her that this was a fate she had already been subjected to and rebelled against. Not yet.

“Your fate? It would have been. Madoka stopped that.”

Homura stared back at her, thoughts racing before spluttering out, “*That* Madoka? The health officer, my… *enemy?*” *The pretty one in the dress?* she added to herself, hoping no one could read her mind; it was a scandalous thought...

“I told you. She was a magical girl too.”

“Then the existence of witches…”

“Means Madoka gave up on saving them.”

Neither girl said anything for a few moments, the hiss of the wind catching both of them off-guard, goosebumps chasing up and down Homura’s arms.

“Miki-senpai…” a voice echoed through the labyrinth, hollow and creepy.

Sayaka was frantic, head spinning wildly as she tried to figure out where the witch was. Butterscotch briquettes lined an obvious path, but one she was reluctant to follow.

*It’s too easy. Charlotte isn’t that simple to defeat.*

“I need to defeat the witch solo if you’re not gonna transform.” she said to Homura.

“I don’t know *how*!”

“Just transform! You *do* know how. It’s like riding a bike, you never really forget. Just *do it*,” she yelped, exasperated. Was she *always* this dense?

She took a deep breath, afraid to disappoint. Same as always, she presumed. She had never learned to ride a bike, in and out of the hospital so many times so young, and she didn’t remember how to transform. Still, she knew what Sayaka had meant…

“I’ll try.”

Homura held her breath, imagining herself covered in glitter and ribbons, like the depictions of magical girls since time immemorial. No dice. *Damn.*

Sayaka gave her a pitying glance, a synthetic smile that Homura struggled to swallow.

“It’s okay. I got this.”

“Wait before you go–”

Sayaka nodded, tapping her heel impatiently.

“Why *does* it matter that Tomoe-san was the one to eliminate this witch*?*”

Sayaka shuffled awkwardly, the echo of her heels colliding with the linoleum of the hospital wing deconstructed. Why *was* she so self-conscious about this?

“Because she has guns and I have *swords*.”

Homura raised an eyebrow, indignant. *Really?* 

“Your magical girl weapon of choice is *swords‽*”

“I didn’t *choose* my weapon, it’s *Kyubey’s fault!”*

*Actually, the weapon of a magical girl is a direct manifestation of the soul. More than one magical girl has described it as an “extension of \[her\]self”.*

Sayaka’s head whipped around, finding the Incubator sitting comfortably on a cinnamon-bun sofa near the outer threshold of the labyrinth.

“What’s your game, Incubator?”

*There is no game, Miki Sayaka. I’m here as an observer, and if permitted, an aide.*

She rolled her eyes, took a breath, and leapt further into the labyrinth. She never expected a straight answer from that cabbity jerk, but it had still been worth a try. As she bounded past a pile of Pyotrs and Polinas, she wondered to herself, where the hell *was* Tomoe Mami anyways?

---

Tomoe Mami was brewing tea. 

Her allowance afforded her such luxuries, and so she indulged. Imports, domestics, luxurious by all accounts, and so she taxed her accounts at times. She spent the days in classes, her nights protecting the city from the ails of the human race. She *deserved* a little treat.

Besides, she got to spread the word today, and that was itself worthy of celebrating. 

Akemi Homura would surely be a difficult convert, but she would come around. After all, even the most ardent of acolytes were tried in their missions.

On the other hand, what to do about Miki Sayaka remained in the forefront of her mind. 

Whether she was a fallen angel in her own right was not a question Mami was equipped to answer. She left the theological implications to God, for only She may judge her. Tomoe Mami’s assignment was simpler, mercifully.

Lady Kaname had declared that Miki Sayaka was to be captured, and then potentially executed. 

“Sayaka-chan is important to me, of course. But she’s also trying to steal *my* Homura-chan away and to ruin the order of things. I don’t want to hurt her, but if she won’t stop… she might doom not just herself, but all magical girls ever. We can’t have that, can we, Mami-san?”

*Mami-san*. She blushed.

The kettle whistled, steam derailing *that* train of thought rather quickly. She slowly got to her feet, her goldenrod socks skating across the wooden floor, the faintest hint of static as she felt her curls attempt to change shape from the charge. A moment later, the strands returned to their mandated spaces, and she was at the counter. 

It was a compelling argument, if only by the grace of her divinity. Good and evil were not so fluid as though one could simply change their allegiances. Mami had come around after a single whisper in her ear.

Tomoe Mami believed in this mission because it was Lady Kaname, and *only* because it was Lady Kaname; it was only idolatry if the object of her adoration weren’t god, after all.

She poured herself a mug and dropped the infuser in, the aroma slowly filling the room. With as much gusto as she could muster, she walked back over to the coffee-table (could it still be called that if she never had anything of the sort?) near the window. She could make dinner, sure, but what was the point of cooking for one? She could survive on lesser nutrition until the deed was done. Such was the obligation of a magical girl.

She watched as the sun dipped below the horizon, feeling the world around her distort as she sipped her tea. She felt the miasma coalesce into something tangible, the ails of humanity manifested as gangly figures, clad in robes as bitrot ate at their synthetic bodies.

Miki Sayaka would have to wait.

---

Sayaka left Homura to her devices, alone. With the Incubator. She didn’t trust him, of course, but she was *already* contracted. It wasn’t like he could contract her again. Right?

Homura held her soul gem up to the faintest of lights overhead, the cold blue of a hospital both familiar and alien in this strange world. 

“You can help her, you know.” Kyubey said, his face not moving a singular inch. Was his mouth even *capable* of opening? The small creature was still sitting in an oversized cinnamon bun, unmistakably still aromatic and flowing.

“How? Do *you* know how I can transform?”

“No, but you do.”

Homura wanted to strangle the Incubator. She wasn’t entirely sure why, but she distinctly remembered attacking the Incubator, over and over. Swinging golf clubs (did she visit a driving range?), firing guns (where did she get *those*‽), and more than once simply strangling the beast to death.

She figured her past self had a pretty good reason to *hate* so intently. Was she actually a hateful person? She hoped not, but the Incubator remained frustratingly smug, confident, and *absolutely useless*. Even if she didn’t know her past-self’s reasoning for the murder, she was fairly confident that she was justified in her absolute loathing of *this* Incubator.

The conversation seemed to drag on and on, minutes turning into hours, still no sign of Sayaka-chan. For transforming, for helping her, the Incubator suggested that she simply “assert her soul over her body” as it was a “mere extension”. As *if* it was that simple! 

He claimed to be there as an aide, but with each passing moment it felt like he was there to antagonize her and little else. If she was being cynical, she might be so bold as to say that his suggestions were *less* than neutral. He prattled off comments so obtuse, so nonspecific as to actually be *harmful.*

*I don’t understand why you’re having such difficulty, Akemi Homura.*

“Me either, Kyubey…”

*I could force you to transform, if that’s something you want.*

“Is that something you can do?”

*Of course. It would involve direct manipulation of your sou—*

“No.” 

She trembled. She wasn’t even sure why she was so repulsed. Kyubey was annoying, sure, but was he malicious? Surely Sayaka-chan would have been more upset if he were? Would she have used those swords to cut him in half?

Homura stomped off, leaving the Incubator curled up on the cinnamon-bun, utterly indifferent to the slather of frosting absolutely coating his fur. (How was he okay with that either‽ It must feel *awful*, and how would he clean himself off? He didn’t have thumbs!)

Her footsteps echoed as she continued deeper into the labyrinth, her wandering leading her to a relatively quiet corner of the nightmarish hospital. Homura slowly slid down the wall, curling up in the corner between “radiology” and “phlebotomy”. The signs were, mercifully, labeled both in 	English and those strange runic glyphs. She was grateful, if only a little bit, for the lessons she had gotten prior to moving to Mitakihara, even if some of her vocabulary had been gained from less than sterile materials outside of school.

When *else* was she going to use that particular skill?  In a nightmarish distortion world, of course!

She hoped Sayaka-chan was okay, but wasn’t brave enough to charge in to offer aid.  Not yet. But Sayaka was not the quiet type. She’d have heard a–

Sayaka was screaming telepathically.  It was quiet, at the farthest reaches of telepathy. (How *did* that telepathy work anyways?) Why hadn’t she heard it before‽ Her skin crawled at the thought, she was crying for help and she hadn’t *heard*.  What kind of friend *was* she?

She quietly sobbed, praying no one could hear her.  What was she going to *do?* 

Something. She had to do *something*, something quick. What felt the most magical focus on memories…  What felt the mos— the memory of the wraith fight. Of *course!*

She focused on that strange memory, of her with a bow, her field of vision subsumed by the wraith. She wasn’t sure what it smelled like, but she knew it must be asphyxiating. She could *feel it,* dragging her down, dukkha incarnate, literal and metaphysical. The pressure in her head mounted, matching the way her heart felt, that itch intensifying into a singular precise emotion, indescribable, that flooded her.

She transformed.

A knee-length skirt kept afloat with a petticoat adorned her lower half, white trim on a charcoal ruffle. As she studied the fabric, she found countless geometric patterns, all of them distinct from one another. She followed the pattern up the skirt and onto her torso, the pattern continuing to not-quite-tesselate. In the altered colorspace of the labyrinth, she felt the texture of the fabric against her skin before she ever saw it.

A monochromatic ruffle on her chest masked the shape of her body, not even hinting at the surgical scar where her greatest weakness lay beating underneath. It wasn’t flashy colors or bold outfit choices. Even the shoes were relatively simple, chunky heels giving her the slightest edge in height. 

It was flattering, it was understated, and it was nothing like what she had imagined. 

It was hers, and it was perfect.  
---

Her reverie, her joy, was so rudely interrupted by a flood of memories, a flood of *experiences*, hitting like a tsunami. Mourning Sayaka-chan in part, memories of terminated timelines, destroyed by a wish made prior, and a prayer made in confidence; in a belief of miracles. 

She had not only watched, but had *tried to stop* Sayaka’s self-destructive spiral, countless attempts to halt her decay, failing fruitlessly every time. She could only recall her horror the first time she watched her would-be friend turn into something *so* much worse than a wraith.

Homura barreled towards the center of the labyrinth, leaping over striped oversized mouselike creatures, dodging nurse outfit-adorned simulacra of people wearing the same nightmarish face. She managed to manifest a bow, picking a few of the familiars off as she got closer to the center of the labyrinth. 

It announced her presence, sure, but at this point? Their heads were patterned like bullseyes. It was *target practice.*

At the center of the storm that was the Sweets Witch’s labyrinth, the fabric doll seemed to wince.  Another Polina had been detonated with that violet light, fading from existence.

Sayaka had stopped trying so hard to escape a while ago. Homura would be on her way soon, whether by the ushering of the familiars hurrying her deeper into the maze, or by her own volition.  Besides, her and the witch’s *tea party* had already garnered an unwanted audience. Charlotte had been whispering in her ear, filling her in on the gaps in Madoka’s plan, between tirades of cheesecake and proselytization.

Homura burst through a wafer-crisp door to find Sayaka held in place with licorice ropes, sitting at a table with a small plush doll. The chair rocked slightly as she writhed in place, contorting to face Homura. She surveyed the room, the ceiling glass and expansive, capped by a chandelier suspended from the peak of the dome.  It looked, though she could hardly tell, like it was tied off against some glass knob at the top; were they in a *cake dish?*

For a moment, nobody said anything, not a single uttered syllable.

The doll smiled at her, jagged teeth discolored from countless sweets consumed. Then its head dipped, as if to acknowledge her entrance.

Homura stood there, bow notched with a radiant fire held taut, ready to fire at any moment. She would not be so foolish as to allow it to catch her off guard.

The witch didn’t move, and Homura faltered.

*If witches are so deadly, so horrid, and this one specifically has caused hurt already… Why isn’t she hurting me? Why not hurt Sayaka-chan? Why keep her prisoner?*

Sayaka looked at her, a concerned and bewildered look on her face. She looked like she had seen a ghost. Homura figured that being captured was terrible for her ego, defenders of justice typically do poorly when turned into damsels. 

*Especially* Sayaka.  
   
On the table in front of Sayaka was a feast of sweets, candies and gels, pralines and butterscotch, ice cream and… medicine vials? All of this made sense except the vials of medicine. Then she thought back, about faceless nurses and IV drips, about the P.A. intercom calling for doctors.

In her mind, a thought echoed from what Sayaka had said earlier.

*She used to be a magical girl.*

Homura allowed the magical arrow in her bow to dissolve, a part of her in utter disbelief at what she was doing. *I shouldn’t. This is irresponsible. I’d never forgive myself if–*

“Do you intend to kill me? Or Miki-san?”

She held a firm grip on her bow. The doll squirmed for a moment, before finally shaking her head no.  
*Take her at her word. Trust her. She means well.*

Homura lowered the bow even further, allowing her grip to loosen.

*Why am I so sure of this? Have I truly lost my mind, even in a world of magical girls?*

“Do you know who I am, who either of us are? Do you know who *you* are?”

Sayaka was dumbfounded. Homura hadn’t *ever* been so forgiving to a witch. She had killed them dispassionately, all in Madoka’s name. In all the timelines she bore witness to, Homura had never shown such vulnerability in the face of a witch. The next closest thing…

*I’m sorry, Miki-san.*

All the while, the ribbon in her hair was a stark white.

The doll’s mouth opened, nonsense spilling out of it as kanji and katakana, runes and latin letters alike drifted up and bumped against the glass of the dome.

“Bebe, am Bebe, tell, story, tell, story!”

“You want us to tell you a story? I’m going to let Miki-san out, if that’s all-right?” Homura said, not quite asking permission. She figured the doll wouldn’t mind if they showed they “wanted” to stay.

Homura ripped through the licorice ropes with ease, Sayaka rising quickly, hugging Homura in an instant.

*I knew you could do it.*

“Bebe tell *you* story.” the stuffed doll babbled, Sayaka and Homura sitting down at the table. Neither had made a conscious choice to do so, licorice ropes or not, and yet they stayed put. 

The witch had invited them in, and they accepted the invitation. *Damn.*

Homura gulped, unsure if she made the right choice. Her and her stupid impulses, her guilt beginning to eat at her.  Damning optimism.  If they died, it was *entirely* her fault; They were entirely at the witch’s mercy.

Sayaka watched in horror as the faceless nurses, the strange dog-things, all slowly surrounded the dome. A few even took seats on the outer edge, the erstwhile knight unsure if those seats had been there prior. The nurses even extinguished their trademark cigarettes, the beasts sitting down obediently. 

It seemed they were here for the story too.

In an infinitely small moment, the witch in the chair opposite Sayaka was replaced, an otherwise ordinary-looking girl sitting in its place. 

The girl that was the doll cradled herself, the fabric of her skin under tension as fingers held steadfast. A girl with stark white hair and amber eyes, two small pigtails held in place with scrunchies (barely). A girl whose middle finger bore the same silver band as Miki Sayaka, as Akemi Homura.  

“Akemi-kami, Miki-senpai, I’d hope you both listen well. This *is* your story after all.”  
   
Homura rolled her eyes, if slightly involuntarily. Was this *really* how this was going to go?

“I take it you’re Momoe Nagisa then?”

“At your service, Akemi-kami. At your service.”

---

“Once upon a time, there were two magical girls.”

“Once upon a time? Really? This isn’t a fairytale.”

“It is to some.”

She grumbled, rolling her eyes. Sayaka-chan was *not* happy with storytime apparently. Maybe because she’d heard it already? Oh well, a recap couldn’t hurt, could it?

“*Once upon a time*, there were two magical girls. They were nothing alike, except that they were, more than they could possibly know.  Like so many before them, they worked together to fend off the evils of the night.”

“This isn’t Sailor Moon, Charlotte. Can it!”

She pouted, crossing her arms in a mighty huff.

“Sayaka-chan, stop interrupting!” she griped, a nurse fitting a licorice rope over her mouth.

*Rude.*  

Sayaka shot daggers at the witch, shrugging them off with the expert ignorance of a child.  She *was* a child, right?

“These girls worked together, and like so many of their peers, the bonds of friendship evolved into something more.  This was not a happy story though, not at first.  One of the girls died a horrid death, and the other made a wish that set her down a path the likes of which would alter the course of all of history.”

Homura was enthralled. She knew that the girl was inevitably heading towards saying she was one of those girls; she wasn’t subtle in calling her Akemi-kami. It still felt wrong, and yet, the story continued to be compelling, more than compelling.  It felt disturbingly familiar.

But did she *want* to be Madoka’s enemy, if this story was recalled in full?  What would it *mean* to become her enemy?  Could her heart stomach it?

“One girl watched as the other sacrificed herself to save all that she loved, over and over and over. Each time she tried, each time she failed, and so she began to fall apart. In the end, she was saved not by her own hand, but by her beloved changing the very circumstances of her demise. She stopped *existing.*”

Sayaka’s eyes went wide, realizing in an instant Nagisa was going to cover the *whole story* if permitted. Oh *no.* A small mercy perhaps, Homura interrupted as well.

“Nagisa-san, I don’t understand something. Why did you not kill me? Are you not still a witch?”

Her eyes were weary, a weight on them well beyond her years (or was she older, her appearance another deception?)

“I am, but I am indebted to you.”

Homura’s head tilted, her eyes still glittering from tears. Her voice, scarcely a whisper.

“What?”

“Once upon a time, my mother was very sick. She was in the hospital, and I visited her frequently. But this isn’t a happy story. She wasn’t a good mother. I was upset, and she was abusive. More than once a girl in the hospital offered comfort, a hug, a kindness. She was stuttering and anxious, and still tried to support me all the same..”

Homura didn’t believe her. Would she have done that? It was a possibility but so was the sky falling tomorrow. It didn’t mean it was likely. Besides, surely she would *remember* such a thing, right?

“I have no memory of that happening.”

“And yet I remember it. And how *are* your memories doing, Akemi-kami? Do you remember how this story ends yet?”

Homura merely fumed.

“I owe you. I owed you then, I owe you now. While you gave me comfort in my life, you’ve become the head of something so much bigger than yourself. Your actions here and now demonstrate that she was right, that it was all for a good reason. Her choice was just.”

“What do you mean?”

“The version of you that you’re unsure of, the one from *before.* She killed me to save innocents. Because another magical girl failed. She saved them.”

Homura nervously shuffled in her chair.

“I wasn’t sure how to defeat you. A witch isn’t the same as her familiars.”

“And yet you also weren’t *stealthy.* You typically kept an element of surprise. You didn’t even know your *magic*.”

“So I was headstrong and foolish”, she said coldly. 

“I disagree.”

“Disagree!?”

The girl waggled her finger, Homura struggling to stomach that the alleged story of her life was being mediated by a girl who sassed her so.  Who *raised* this child!?

“I think, more than anything, you were optimistic. A magical girl may turn into a witch, but some part of you recognized it as growing up. Magical girls *mature* into witches.”

Sayaka rolled her eyes. How many times had *she* given the same speech, the same explanation? Did those girls buy it any more than Homura was? Of cou-

“But Miki-san described your maturity as agony. A mercy killing! What type of maturity is agony!?”

“Isn’t growing up always uncomfortable?” 

One of the familiars squeaked, a nurse putting out a cigarette on its lid.

Nagisa cast a side-eye at the familiar, her “coworkers” hauling her out of the dome post-haste. The hospital might be a smoking zone, but her sanctuary at the epicenter of her labyrinth was a distinct no smoking zone. She could hardly handle the smell nowadays.

She continued with the story.

“When the time came for her beloved to be taken away to heaven, the heaven she created, the magical girl rebelled. She ripped her goddess in half, stealing part of her away, to make her mortal again and vulnerable.”

Homura’s eyes began to tear.

“It was fragile. She made a world where she could be happy, where she could live free of the curse that she saw being a magical girl as.”

*We’ll always be together from now on…*

“It all fell apart, she couldn’t hold on. She took off too much, and it was too much to bear. Her face was stolen by an interloper and, well...”

*I’ve waited so long for this...*

“In the end–”

The sound of breaking glass filled the dome as the witch’s body slumped before dissolving, both the fabric doll *and* the human girl in equal measure. Amber shards of stone lay scattered on the ground around the chair, the ring was attached to spread out in a bloody smear. 

Sayaka screamed, struggling to stand. Homura jumped to her feet and manifested her bow before Sayaka had a chance to even notice. She pulled it taut, violet flames licking the side of her hand as she let loose, the glass dome shattering as Tomoe Mami descended into the already dissolving labyrinth.
