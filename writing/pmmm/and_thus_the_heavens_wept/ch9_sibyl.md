“Why th’ hell are we *here?”*

Hitomi bid the driver adieu, the school gates locked, the construction cranes still moving in the glittering night.

“I... don’t know for sure.”

Kyoko considered simply jumping the fence, but thought better of it. Those cameras might still be around–

“Maybe I can help answer your questions.” Madoka replied quietly, the girls spinning around to find her standing behind them.

“How th’ hell did you–”

“Kaname-san.” Hitomi bowed, Madoka grimacing in response.

“Please don’t, Hitomi-chan.”

Hitomi looked at her, her face colder than Kyoko had seen on her...ever.

“Please don’t be that way, Hitomi-chan... I know you’ve been reading, but you know I’m doing this for a good reason.”

“We’ve been reading alright... ‘bout you and yer’ damned prophecy.”

The stick of Rocky swung in her mouth, the end crunching in rage.  Madoka’s weary eyes narrowed, sure Kyoko could do the same to steel or stone all the same.  

“It’s not really... *mine*?  Well, kinda.  It’s the one Mikuni-san foretold.”

“Foretold!?  Why th’ hell can’t we just choose our own ending!?”

“I’m trying Kyoko-chan.” Madoka sighed.  “I really am...”

Hitomi shoved the book into Kyoko’s arms and stomped across the catwalk to mere inches from Madoka’s face.

“You listen here. I have *had it* with your absurd devil visage! You are no such thing, and neither is she! What you did, what you *both did*, you did out of *love*.  What happened to telling people to hold onto hope!?”

Kyoko’s eyes widened.  What the hell is seaweed-brain doing!?

“Hey, hey, Hitomi, stop.  Stop! I said—”

“Tell the truth. You’re not a devil, and you know it.”

A creeping smile appeared on Madoka’s face.  It wasn’t like she had a *choice* when she made such a show of it.  The stage demanded her be vindictive.

“Oh... you really do want me to tell the truth then, Hitomi-chan?”

She nodded, fire in her eyes.

“Why don’t you go ask Sayaka-chan what eternity looks like then Kyoko-chan?”

Madoka clapped her hands only once, and Kyoko found herself staring at a familiar door, the elegant katakana on the nameplate all the confirmation she needed.

“Hitomi, why did she send us to Mami’s—”

“Hitomi?”

Kyoko spun to find herself alone, her partner-of-the-hour conspicuously absent.

*This is some bullshit,* she thought to herself as she knocked on the door.

It popped open on its own, Kyoko seeing the goldenrod through the crack as she realized in an instant that the aquamarine blob on top of her was little more than the girl she sought so desperately.

*Sayaka.*

Mami tossed her shirt back on as she stumbled to the door, peeking through it as she surveyed the landscape.

“Something wrong Mami-san?” Sayaka said, her voice raised from across the apartment.

“No, no Miki-san.  I just thought I heard–” she trailed off.

Her eyes cast downwards as she murmured to herself.

“Kyoko...”

On the ledge three floors below, Sakura Kyoko was sobbing, trying to be as quiet as she possibly could be.

She snapped back to the gates of Mitakihara Secondary, tears cascading down her face as Madoka looked at her with contempt in her eyes.

“Did you get your answer Kyoko-chan?”

Kyoko didn’t respond, Hitomi frantically pulled tissues out of her jacket.  

“What did you *do* to her!?  This was wholly unnecessary!  Cruelty for *no reason*!”

Madoka didn’t dignify her outrage with a response either.

“My end goal is peace Hitomi-chan, Kyoko-chan.   I want us to not have to fight anymore.  I got so tired of watching girls like us suffer *so* much... and even when Homura-chan’s rebellion happened, we had the chance to change it!  And so... I did. What choice did I have? It was never a choice at all, really.”

Hitomi realized more than anything, she had to *stall for time*. She needed Homura to find a way back, so the story could continue.

“Then tell us how that went because, well... it’s not something I’d remember.” Hitomi smiled blithely.

Madoka nodded once before kneeling next to Kyoko. She put her palm under her chin and cradled her head on her chest.

“Before I do... Kyoko-chan?”

She sniffled something of an acknowledgement as Madoka fixed the bow in her hair, steady fingers as she redid the braids in the redhead’s barely-tamed locks. 

“It’s not your love being denied by Law. It’s mine.  You still have a chance to be with her again.  You have to *try* Kyoko-chan.”

“*Madoka*, stop antagonizing her!”

“I’m sorry, Hitomi-chan, but this is the way the story has to go.”
