### Higanbana

—

Madoka opened the door to the ever-present babbling of her younger brother.

“Maoka, Ma-oka!” he chirped, Madoka picking him up, gently bouncing him as she kept him close.

“Hi Tatsuya-kun, how are you?” she cooed, her parents watching on in minor amusement.

Tomohisa was, unexpectedly, sitting on the couch, watching something on the television. A nice change of pace, off his feet for once, Madoka thought to herself. He usually spent his time in the garden, but the small flurry of rain clouds had made the home garden too risky for someone of his “advanced age”. He scoffed.

Mama sat at the dinner table, buried deep in take-home work; multiple quarters worth of reports sat waiting for review. She had balked at the workload while away at the office once - “Oh, but it’s the digital age! Why can’t we just get a program to do this?” - to which her *very* traditional boss simply added another handful of reports on top of the pile.

She was nursing a whiskey glass, entirely unrelated to the work stress. Allegedly.

“Madoka, how was school?” her father asked, turning the television off to give his full attention to his favorite (and only) daughter.

Madoka, still holding her brother close, shrugged weakly.

“It was okay. I’m tired though.”

“Sounds like you’re finally growing up!” her father quipped, Madoka feigned a smile as she put her now-squirming brother back onto the carpet.

“I’m going to go to my room, if that’s okay.”

“Sounds good. Dinner is in an hour, if you’re feeling hungry.”

Madoka’s back was already turned towards them, the door closing with a quiet click.

Junko got up from the table slowly, both the circumstances and her inebriation demanding deliberation. She tip-toed over to the couch, and sat down next to her husband.

Both Kaname parents shared a look, and just breathed for a moment.

“She looks like she’s not sleeping…”

“Yeah…”

“And she’s so…”

“Depressed?”

Junko buried her face in her hands, letting out a heavy sigh.

“I was hoping you wouldn’t say it.”

“It’s like we landed back in Mitakihara and all of her joy just… vanished.”

Neither of them say a word for a moment, Tatsuya stumbling towards the couch, toys in hand. Tomohisa picked him up and bounced him on his knee, distracted for a moment by the boy's happy babbling, but the dissonance was sobering.

“Should we offer to take her to the doctor?”

“Not like she’d take us up on it… She’s too *independent* now*.* And well, we want her to be, to a point.  But I don’t want her getting hurt because she’s trying to do things on her own.”

“That stubbornness runs in the family…” 

His legs stop bouncing, letting out a quiet breath. He wasn’t liking *this part* of fatherhood.

*Sigh.*

The devil upstairs stopped listening and closed her eyes, focusing on controlling her erratic breathing instead. Extraneous, superfluous, *wasteful.* She would never be able to achieve salvation for magical girls if she wasn’t committed to it, instead of merely going through the motions. Still, it was better than eavesdropping on her *parents.* 

She had discarded her humanity, and then her *divinity*, and now here she was, focusing on her breathing, on the rise and fall, having a panic attack like some mundane *thing*. She would never hear the end of it.

She hadn’t meant to eavesdrop, honest. She wanted to respect their privacy, really, but the devil has ears–

She shook her head, as if that would dispel the constant whispering, and began to undress; a shower was calling to her. She had no need, strictly speaking, but then again, neither did humans. It’s just *nice* to smell nice.

She tossed aside her uniform, grateful that the scars on her arms were hidden by the prim and proper sleeves. Her parents wouldn’t have a chance to see the consequences of her actions for a *very* long time. Besides, she could always just erase their mem—

**No.** She would face the consequences, even if it meant them learning a horrible truth. 

She couldn’t, she *wouldn’t* try to hide or escape from the undeniable reality; that their daughter was a good-for-nothing devil, who broke promises and lied to her very best friend.

She turned the shower as high as it would go. It was a meager penance for her transgressions.

She took a look in the mirror while she waited for the shower to warm up and surveyed the girl in front of her. The same dusty pink hair remained in the mirror, the same smile on the girl’s face when she bothered to wear it. But the eyes, her *eyes*, they looked so empty. Where was the joy, the whimsy? All she bore witness to was an exhaustion beyond words. 

She really *did* look like death, huh?  

*How did Homura-chan do it? Was it really “just” love….?*

Was being a devil always so hard?

She shook her head, and opened the faucet in the sink. Cold, warmer, lukewarm, hm, maybe a touch too hot, *screak*. 

As the shower finally began to give off the faintest wisps of steam, she pulled the stopper in the sink and closed the faucet.  She dipped her pinky in, knowing full well it was comfortable; it wasn’t for her. 

She took her earcuff off, gently placing it on the counter as it sprang to life. The animated earcuff scrambled across the stained wooden surface of the counter and stumbled into the sink, clearly overjoyed at the warmth.

It gently preened itself, Madoka relieved to finally let the little bird stretch its clipped wings and enjoy itself in the sink-turned-birdbath. She smiled weakly as she watched it, hoping the joy of *some* part of her beloved would reach the whole of her. 

A small mercy in this cursed world they’d made together.

*Why would she love a coward like me?*

Madoka climbed into the shower, steam billowing around her as she tried not to think about how the near-boiling stream *should* hurt her, how she *deserved* for it to hurt. The water cascaded down her body, a half-hearted attempt to wash those vile thoughts away, but there truly was no rest for the wicked. 

*What would Homura-chan have done?* 

She stared up at the ceiling, her eyes defocused.

*This. She’d have done what I did.*

As the water coalesced beneath her, one of those cursed memories played out in her mind’s eye, a tragedy set in the ruins of Mitakihara, entirely from the wrong perspective. She had kept those memories safe, kept them close, all to keep them out of *Homura-chan’s* head.

Madoka was helpless to stop the memory as she felt Homura’s finger crush the trigger, a sickening click as tears cascaded down her face and landed in the shower with a splash. The only thing she could hear, knowing full well Homura-chan was screaming, was the sound of her own soul gem shattering.

The doorbell rang.

Madoka was suddenly aware she was heaving, breathy and nearly to the point of vomiting. The doorbell’s sing-song ditty continued, fighting fiercely against the pulsing in her ears, all from the pounding of her stupid, stupid heart. She forced herself to listen carefully through the waterfall.

Her mother had gotten up to get the door, her father grateful as he continued playing with Tatsuya. 

Junko teeter-tottered to the door, finding the figure outside in a moss-tone raincoat, a matching umbrella protecting her hair, still perfectly styled despite the humidity.

“Ah, one of Madoka’s classmates. What’s your name, dear?”

“Shizuki Hitomi, Kaname-sama.”

*Shizuki-san? At home?*

She accepted Junko’s invitation inside, collapsing her raincoat and hooking her umbrella. She wouldn’t be here long (Madoka hoped). Junko disappeared into the house, snaking her way to Madoka’s room, to rap on the door gently.

“Madoka! Shizuki-san is here!”

“I’ll be right out Mama!”

—--

“Hitomi-chan!  What a nice surprise! I haven’t seen you outside of school since…” she puttered, Hitomi taking the reins with ease.

“I believe the last time I had the pleasure of visiting was after one of Kamijou-kun’s recitals.”

“Why not visit more often then?” She smiled, Junko and Tomohisa only marginally impressed that she was managing to keep up her facade in the face of a classmate.

Is *that* what Kazuko hadn’t texted her about their brooding teenage delinquent? She could fake it at school? Junko grimaced, thinking for a moment about her *friend’s* tradition of half-plastered texts about her endless stream of terrible dates, each worse than the last. 

Then again, would she *really* prefer to gossip with that academic trainwreck about her dear daughter? No, not especially. *Damn.*

“Oh, I’ve been busy with my extracurriculars. Tea ceremonies, piano lessons, business classes… it all adds up, unfortunately.”

“You’re quite studious, Hitomi-chan.”

“Thank you, Kaname-san, but I think we both know why I’m here.”

Both girls briefly shared a look as Madoka’s brow furroughed. She didn’t expect *this*. Hitomi doesn’t visit her home, not once in Homura’s loops. What *changed?*

“Mama? Papa? Can Shizuki-san go to my room? She wants to talk about something *private.”*

Hitomi turned to Madoka’s parents and gave them a sympathetic *what-can-you-do* type of look, to which the adults responded in turn the only way a parent could; by engaging in realpolitik of a different class.

Junko replied first,  “Of course. Just don’t be too long.”

Tomohisa continued, “Shizuki-san, will you be staying for dinner?”

“I don’t believe so, no.”

Junko continued “It’s no trouble if you do, sometimes conversations take longer than you think.”

A pregnant pause was the only inclination that Shizuki-san had understood the barely-buried subtext, her smile the same immaculate and unyielding fascia it always was.

“I wouldn’t want to intrude, but thank you very much for even entertaining the notion, Kaname-sama. 

“Oh you don’t have to be so formal, Shizuki-san, I promise!” Junko said, ever the conciliator.

The girls vanished into the twists and turns of the household as Tomohisa returned to dinner prep, chopping vegetables with the mastery of a professional chef. As he did so, he couldn’t help but share a knowing glance with Junko, his wife only returning it with side-eye. 

*Girl problems?*

*She takes after her mother.* 

—--

“Mami-san!” Homura managed to yelp, Sayaka staring intently at the musket trained on her.

*She’s gonna do it. She’s really gonna do it. I can’t go out like this, not again, I—* 

Mercifully, implausibly, the zealot lowered her musket, if only a little.

“I was out hunting wraiths, the miasma had become especially thick near the port. I come to investigate this strange anomaly and, lo and behold, who do I find? A fallen angel and her *thrall.*”

Sayaka grit her teeth, clenched her fists. Sure, she wasn’t being maimed, but was she really just going to *take this* from Tomoe Mami? She didn’t start a damn *speech* like that unless there was more gloating to be done. 

*“*She had kept you both hostage, evidently. No thanks are needed though.”

*Who the hell does she think she is—*

“Miki Sayaka. You are to come with me. Should you try to escape, I *will* kill you. You have no other option.”

She scoffed, rolling her eyes. As if she’d go down without a fi–

The musket fired, the bullet ripping through her abdomen two millimeters above her soul gem.

*When had she—*

She doubled over, a blossom of crimson nipped in the bud as the wound sealed itself. She stumbled back to her feet, swaying like windchimes, her disgust only steeping. Mami continued her tirade.

“I believe I’ve made my point.”

Sayaka fumed, glowered, foamed, seethed, bristled. Homura was barreling towards exhaustion of her vocabulary before her former mentor’s attention suddenly snapped to her.

“Akemi-san. This labyrinth will be dissolving soon. You should leave.”

She gulped nervously, struggling to find the words for a moment.

“I’m not going to *leave* Sayaka-chan!” she ultimately said, carried with an unmistakable air of confidence. Where *had* Mami seen that look before…?

“With respect, Akemi-san. Lady Kaname has given me strict orders to retrieve Miki Sayaka. She has given no such directive for you, but if you *interfere…*”

She spared a single glance at Homura, her posture stiff and unyielding. Tomoe Mami sighed as her ribbons wrapped around Miki Sayaka, bound and held captive for the nth time that day. Sayaka’s eyes looked empty, clearly done with the antics of every would-be heroine that would come her way. 

Even *Charlotte* was a more gracious host, she didn’t feel the need to try and act cool for her *kōhai*. 

Tomoe Mami sauntered over to the spot where the witch, no, where *Momoe Nagisa* had been mere minutes before, gently sifting through the illusion. After a few moments, she plucked out three or four amber shards. She took off her beret, her own soul gem still attached to it, as she dropped the shards of what was once a girl into the void inside her hat.

Homura could only look on, wearing the fear that Tomoe-san expected of her as she nearly drowned in thoughts run amok.

*Since when would the shards of a soul gem persist? Why is she collecting them? To what end is she capturing Miki Sayaka? Why does she seem so unconcerned with me, is it because I’m bad at being a magi—*

As the last vestiges of the labyrinth began to collapse with a synthetic wind, Tomoe Mami’s corset and beret dissolved back into her school uniform, Homura and Sayaka’s attire following suit. Despite this, Sayaka remained tethered to Tomoe Mami and her ribbon, a single golden band around her neck anchoring her to her deft hand. 

Symbolic, obvious, perverse.

Hadn’t she been gagged enough?

Homura stood there, trembling as she choked back tears, struggling to stop them from surfacing as she quietly wheezed. As she reeled trying to reconcile her memories and what she was witnessing, Tomoe Mami vanished into the night, Sayaka vanishing with her. 

Standing alone once more, Homura slowly but surely stopped shaking, those telltale tears on her cheeks drying up. There was far too much at stake to cry for something so small, and yet, even in that moment, she wasn’t sure how fake the tears were.

She was not equipped to deal with the kidnapping of Miki Sayaka just yet, especially given how *woefully* underarmed she felt with only a bow. There were still too many unknowns, too many moving parts. She had to hope that Tomoe Mami believed her trembling, shaking reaction as authentic. 

She was cast in the role of a pathetic, fragile underclassman that wasn’t able to defend her closest (and only) friend. It came naturally, it was as easy as slipping on a jumper; it was her reality. At some point between the hospital, the port, and a few thousand stolen guns though? She just got better at burying that terrified girl.

Homura was still lost in her memories.

Countless scenes within city limits flit through her mind, The same month folded over and over until it felt as though the fabric of her life refused to fold, and they collapsed. How many times had she walked between the homes of her classmates, defeating the same set of witches over and over. 

How many times *had* she seen this city fall to ruin? 

The biggest mystery of all still lingered at the forefront of her mind, the immediate risk of what Kaname Madoka, her alleged enemy, was planning. She *would* find a way to save Sayaka-chan, reverse whatever happened to Tomoe Mami, and to halt the plan of the devil who remained at the epicenter of the universe. 

A thought stirred, irrepressible. Could Kaname Madoka alter memories? A hole that large, that *sharp* and deliberate, couldn’t be anything but intentional, surely.  

Homura would have resolved to go home and make *some* progress on this oddity, but something ate at her, something important.  

She turned back to the space that had been occupied by her would-be confidant, the storyteller, the *sweets witch*, and found herself mumbling to no one in particular.

“Goodbye, Miss Nagisa. And thank you for trying.”

---

Homura stumbled into her apartment, utterly the same as she left it, and yet nothing like it was. She knew exactly where on the floorboards to line up the holoprojector. She knew which police stations had negligent safe practices. And most importantly, she knew how to be a magical girl.

What she *didn’t* know was who Kaname Madoka was.

She collapsed into the armchair she had but a week prior, unsure if she could consider herself the same person. Could she *truly* be the same girl? How much of you has to change to be a new person? 

In the back of her mind, tinny and inauthentic, some part of her mind (as a coping mechanism, clearly) prattled off inane factoids.

{{ *Human bodies replace cells on average every seven years.* }}

Seven years, arbitrary. 

Seven years would make up half her body’s age to date, but that was hardly the whole story. For *some reason* (Sayaka-chan?), she’d repeated the same few months countless times. Why was the *reason* so elusive though?

Would years of memories suffice to be a changed girl? Even if she didn’t have all of them back? And why was she so hesitant to regain them? Was she afraid to find out she was some horrid per—

She bolted towards the window, gulping down the fresh air as she tried to keep the bile down. The taste was still fresh enough to make her completely repulsed by food, so she tried to focus on the challenges thus declared.

She wasn’t exactly fighting anyone right *now*.  Surely if her enemy was truly omniscient, she’d have shown up by now, right? “When you remember, we’ll be enemies.” was quite the declaration, only to be met with relative quiet.

Homura gulped, and dared to take it as a sign of her implied omniscience being limited; what choice did she have?

She remembered the feeling of her outfit on her skin, smooth and coarse all at once.

She walked to the door, turned the lock, then the deadbolt; she wanted privacy, even if she was decently sure it’d do little to deter the most determined of snoops. She strode over to the other side of the room and went to close the curtains, hesitating. This time, she made *sure* to flip the window’s latch shut.

She stood in the center of the living room, roughly equidistant from the few material things she cared about; a meager manga collection, a plush she may or may not have been given by one of the nurses at the hospital, and earrings that she was told were the majority of her inheritance. (She wasn’t sure she could even get her ears pierced.)  
   
She descended into her own memory again, recounting the first time she ever donned the regalia of a magical girl juxtaposed to the *last* time she’d done so. Different costumes, different circumstances (she presumed, it was a little difficult to tell what had happened), and yet… the same person?

Homura had this nagging feeling that *something* anchored her, even beyond the tragedy that she had just witnessed, of the *coldblooded murder* that Tomoe Mami had committed, even if it was to “save her” from a witch. What was it that gave her continuity, that connected her to the girl she was, and the girl she will be?

What connected her to humanity at all, for that matter? — and at this thought she recalled an all-too-familiar struggle, and the irony defining her very existence: that she should be so distant from humanity, but dwelling deep in the most human of emotions.

She shivered, unsure quite why.  *Maybe someone just walked over my grave... or is that just a dumb superstition?* 

She transformed again, letting the outfit flow in the dim lighting of her apartment. The dress still hugged her body *just* right, she could move it out of the way or avoid brushing into it as easily as if it were moving a limb.

*The weapon of a magical girl is a direct manifestation of the soul.*

She supposed, perhaps for the first time, that her attire was much the same. Perhaps that’s how Tomoe Mami’s ribbons worked? But she can’t do ribbons, she had–

The bow. She summoned it in an instant, finally getting a chance to inspect it under less tense circumstances. She admired the cherry blossoms on the summit of the bow, a bud except when she fired it. The knurls and whorls in the staff portion felt just right in her hand, as if it was grown just for her.

If it was an extension of herself, it must have been.  *Right?*

She pulled it closer to her face, her eyes transfixed on the two gems embedded in the grip. Their shape and faceting made them clear duplicates of the gem on her hand when she was in costume, but her own shone purple. The stones on the bow, by contrast… they glittered pink.

*Why were they pink?*

With a huff, Homura collapsed into the bed in the corner of the room. The earlier rush of excitement at learning  that *she of all girls* was a magical girl was fading further and further away, replaced with  dread, shock, horror. She rolled over in bed, suddenly aware that the sutures in her chest didn’t seem to ache even a little bit. 

A knot in her gut tightened, her heart ready to burst in her chest. Her face turned flush, her mouth whispering a name into her pillow. 

“Madoka… What have you done….?”

—--

Hitomi-chan, couldn’t this have waited until school?”

“He tried again earlier tonight.”

Madoka froze.

“I don’t know what you mean, Hitomi-chan. Kajimou-kun?”

“I think you do, *akuma.*” Her voice was quiet, but she emphasized each syllable. *A-ku-ma.* A devil*.* Ah, it was *that* type of house-call.

Madoka briefly flirted with the idea of direct memory alteration, a form of minor-delay telepathy. No, direct memory alterations felt gross…. Hm…

Whispering it is.

“He tried again?”

“It was just like Miki-san had said. He talked of karmic potential and destiny, about entropy. All of it.”

“And you didn’t, did you?”

She shook her head, Madoka relaxing as she processed the words. *Thank goodness.*

“That’s for the best, Hitomi-chan. The longer you put it off, the better.”

Such an idea would have been treasonous in any other residence of a magical girl, but Kaname Madoka *wasn’t* a magical girl; she was a different beast entirely.

“I take it you mean that for a good reason, right?”

“Mhmm.”

“What was it that you had said on the walk to school the other day?”

“You should be discreet. If the existence of magic isn’t kept secret, it could disappear.”

Neither girl said anything for a moment, Hitomi clearly considering her next words carefully. She looked at Madoka with resolve in her eyes. A brave front, a steeled determination Madoka knew *too* well. Oh *no.*

“Kaname-san, I don’t believe that avoiding this topic does anyone good here.  To be curt… I’m your backup plan, aren’t I?”

*Well, if she knows…*

“I’m sorry, Hitomi-chan. I didn’t want you to have to get involved.”

“It’s okay, Kaname-san, it’s a bit funny in truth. The way I see it, nothing would actually change.”

—-

Homura entered the class with a sense of incoherence she couldn’t quite shake. She hadn’t slept well the night before. She was too *nervous*, too on edge that something might go bump in the night. 

Sayaka-chan didn’t show up to school, and while Homura didn’t end up checking the classes for the grade above, she was fairly confident she wouldn’t find Tomoe Mami.

“Akemi-san, Akemi-san, did you hear?”

“Oh my *gosh*, there’s gossip that Miki-san has a crush on Kajimou-san!”

“Oh, but everyone knows *Shizuki-san* has first dibs, they’ve been taking such tender care of each other for years!”

Homura sat down and tried to tune out the droning of the regular trio. Had they *always* been this exhausting? No, she reneged, several more years of experience made their behavior less cutesy, and far more irritating.

“How do you think this is going to end, Good-For-Nothing?”

Homura’s head whipped around to face Minori, reaching for–-for—for—

“What did you say?”

Minori jumped out of her seat slightly, shrinking as Homura loomed over her.

“I said who do you think Kajimou-san will accept the confession of? Miki-san or Shizuki-san?”

Homura’s mind reeled as she tried to understand why she heard *Good-For-Nothing.* She knew the name well, she called herself good-for-nothing more often than she’d ever admit. But Minori wouldn’t say that to her, not out of the blue.

*Right?*

“Oh, drop it Minori. I think *Akemi-san* has her own stake in that relationship’s path.”

“What do you mean?” she mumbled, Koito whispering into her ear as recognition flashed across her face.

Homura’s cheeks turned a shade of pink that rarely showed up on her. Despite putting her hair down, and despite years of memories of being a magical girl, the mere *implication* of sapphic yearning was sufficient to reduce her to a stuttering mess.

She turned back to the front of the class, eager for Saotome-sensei to provide *any* distraction. Even then though, she was granted a more pleasant than usual reprieve. Rather than her weekly drivel on the quality (or lack thereof) on her dates, she was instead excitedly sharing her plans for a field trip.

A fieldtrip, Homura wondered; was this just her trying to scout out her next date on a school outing’s budget? It wasn’t like the budget was *enormous*, but surely Saotome-sensei was paid enough for this, right? 

Homura shook her head. No, this isn’t the time to get caught up in class politics and her teacher’s love life. No, something *else* was clearly wrong. The capture of Miki Sayaka was one thing, but… hm. Her head was still aching, countless memories overflowing. 

How was she supposed to deal with this? Her throat felt like she hadn’t had water in centuries, coarse and dry. It was like she had sand in her mouth… She tried to focus on the ramble, praying it would be more tolerable than her own accursed body awareness.

They were going to the Mitakihara Conservatory. To look at the flowers. And the paired lesson was on… *lycoris radiata.* Spiderlilies.

Kazuko continued onwards about how they symbolized rebirth, and death. About how in some faiths the higanbana is symbolic of the distant shore, over the rainbow bridge, and plenty of other malapropisms. She even tried to connect it to local history, about how the Japanese variant can only reproduce by bulb division rather than traditional pollination.

She suggested that the class might very well have one or more interesting papers on the symbology or literary implication of such a flower, where its existence can only be as a duplicate of another.

Homura blinked. *Good-For-Nothing* as a name was one thing, and now this? 

A distinct outline was beginning to form in her mind, one she really didn’t want to evaluate, even for a moment. Starburst fragments of memory once dispersed doubled back like shrapnel, less-than-fatal to any organ but the heart. 

Tragedy after tragedy, each more agonizing than the last, the souring taste of grief seeds like needles and acid. More misery than she could possibly stomach all at once, memories that made her jaw hurt.

One memory where her head was sliced in half, her mind replaced with a bouquet of the poisonous flower.

Homura shuddered to her feet, not quite asking to be excused. She stumbled out of the classroom, the air of gloom following her, dead girl walking. Minori waited for Saotome-sensei to gesture wildly at the anatomy of the spiderlily, to ramble about the sociological significance of the flower, and then ducked out of the room to follow her.

She feebly kicked the bathroom door open, her arms outstretched as she burst into the stall. Her vision filled with stars, spinning. In a single violent motion, Homura vomited that rose-hue sand into the toilet, all while the world distorted in a polychromatic smear.

—

A quiet knock at the door stirred her, brushing crumbs off of her hoodie as she stood up.

*Figures she’d show up now. Can’t a girl get some damn beauty sleep?*

She cracked the door, quietly, carefully, as if not to hint to the interloper that she had a spear just out of sight. She tried not to think about how grumpy Momo-chan would be if she woke her.

“Hello Sakura-san.”
